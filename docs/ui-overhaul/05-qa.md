# Tabletop UI overhaul: final QA handoff

## Verdict

Approved for handoff after three browser-found UI defects were fixed and
retested. The supported two-through-six-player tabletop, primary placement and
contest interactions, reveal presentation, turn emphasis, shared playback, and
device-local mute all worked in the exercised Chrome sessions. `make check`
passes after the QA fixes.

No game rule, API route, shared protocol, scoring behavior, or generated
coverage was changed by QA.

## Browser setup and scope

- Tested the Vite client against the real Express development API in Chrome at
  1280x720/1024x768 landscape, 768x1024 portrait, 390x844, and 320x568.
- Used separate local Vite origins as isolated browser clients and API-driven
  peers for deterministic multiplayer actions. Rooms with 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, and
  7 players were started against the real server.
- The active OS/browser preference was dark mode. Reduced motion and increased
  contrast were not active in the available browser session.
- Browser screenshots were visually inspected at the two-player waiting,
  placement, contested reveal, six-player, phone reveal, seven-player fallback,
  and token-drag states. No screenshot files were added to the repository.

## Manual scenarios and results

### Two-player game flows

Room `ZHAC` used Alice in the browser and Bob as a deterministic API-driven
peer. More than two complete rounds were exercised.

1. **Wrong active placement, correct contested steal — passed.** Bob placed
   2008 before 1975. Alice selected the unclaimed `After 1975` gap, first by
   click and then entirely by keyboard after Escape cancellation. Selection
   left the server vote list empty and kept the visible token count at two.
   Explicit `Confirm steal · costs 1 token` submitted the vote, changed the
   server-confirmed count to one, and moved the phase to pending reveal. Reveal
   showed Bob as `Incorrect`, Alice as `Correct`, highlighted the correct gap,
   retained Bob's pre-insertion target timeline, and awarded Alice the card.

2. **Correct active placement — passed.** Alice used a real pointer drag from
   the mystery card to `After 2008` for the 2014 card. The drop produced a ghost
   and enabled Confirm without sending a request; Confirm alone advanced the
   phase. Bob passed. Alice entered an optional title/artist guess and revealed.
   The answer face showed title, artist, authoritative year, the correct marker,
   and the pre-insertion timeline. The `No bonus token` path gated `Next turn`
   until judgment was complete.

3. **Playback and second bonus path — passed.** As the active player, Alice
   played and paused the track, sought while playing, observed the one-poll
   `Paused — waiting for everyone to catch up…` lock and automatic resume, then
   sought while paused and verified it stayed paused. Playback continued through
   confirmed placement, Bob's pass, pending reveal, and the revealed card. The
   `Yes, claim a token` path incremented the server-confirmed token pile and
   exposed Next turn. The next song mounted at 0:00 and remained paused until its
   new active player acted.

4. **Local mute — passed.** Mute applied while shared playback remained playing,
   emitted no game log/action change, updated `aria-pressed`, surfaced `Muted on
   this device`, and persisted after reload. Followers retained disabled
   transport controls while seeing shared progress.

5. **Spoiler audit — passed.** Before reveal, the mystery card's DOM and
   accessibility snapshot contained only the mystery face/audio controls; the
   current title, artist, year, song id, manifest entry, and answer face were not
   mounted. They appeared only after the server entered reveal.

### Drag, keyboard, and action semantics

- Real pointer card drag and token drag both selected exact timeline gaps after
  the QA fixes below. Dropping did not submit an action; explicit confirmation
  did.
- Direct click/tap-equivalent gap selection, Enter pickup, Home/End navigation,
  Enter selection, Escape cancellation, and Confirm were exercised. The live
  announcement reported pickup/selection/confirmation.
- Claimed gaps were disabled and named with claimant plus `unavailable`.
  Confirm buttons stayed disabled without a selection. Pass remained available.
- Active placement and steal confirmation immediately produced the server phase,
  vote status, marker, and token-count changes; no optimistic vote or token spend
  was observed.

### Seating, emphasis, and responsive layout

- Real rooms with 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 players rendered every expected zone. A
  bounding-rectangle audit found zero player/player or player/draw-stack
  intersections for every supported count. Horizontal/vertical lane orientation
  matched the seat map, text remained upright, and the viewer occupied the
  bottom seat.
- The six-player screenshot showed all six fixed zones, the center stack, coin
  piles, long-name truncation, correct clockwise placement, and no collision.
- Turn emphasis was verified from computed styles: the active zone had full
  saturation and a transparent veil; a spectator had `saturate(0.7)` and the
  felt-colored veil. In a contest window, the viewer's actionable zone and the
  active target were both emphasized.
- At 320x568, 390x844, and 768x1024 the document had no horizontal overflow,
  the table-scroll hint was visible, and a fresh load centered horizontally and
  aligned the internal table viewport toward the viewer/center. At 390x844 the
  room, mute, instruction, revealed card, active player, and Next turn action
  were simultaneously visible. At 1024x768 the full board fit without an
  internal scroll.
- A real seven-player room rendered all seven players in the ordered list
  fallback with no page-wide horizontal overflow.

### Regression observations

- Lobby create/join/start flows, room code, host/you labels, vote/pass badges,
  event-log disclosure, and missing-action loading states remained present.
- App console inspection found no client errors or warnings. Logged warnings
  came only from the browser-control extension.

## QA fixes made and retested

1. **Reveal face hidden after flip.** Added `transform-style: preserve-3d` to
   the flip wrapper. Before the fix, answer metadata existed in the accessibility
   tree but the striped draw-stack back remained visible after the transition.
2. **Reveal metadata obscured by transport.** Added the missing reusable
   visually-hidden utility, marked the reveal draw stack, and compacted/lowered
   its audio controls. Title, artist, and year are now visibly readable while
   playback status and progress remain available.
3. **Card drag source intercepted by the audio slider.** Positioned the 44px
   `Drag card` handle on the unobstructed top edge of the mystery card. A repeat
   pointer drag selected the intended boundary gap instead of seeking audio.
4. **Token drag source mostly covered by the name plate.** During an actionable
   contest only, the player's pile now lifts above the plate. Hit testing reaches
   the full token button, and the repeated pointer drag selected the intended
   active-timeline gap while leaving the count unchanged until confirmation.

## Automated verification

- `make check` — passed after QA fixes: 5 test files, 28 tests, zero lint
  warnings, successful shared/web/API builds.
- `git diff --check` — passed.

## Coverage constraints and remaining low-risk items

The following were not reproduced manually in the available browser session;
they are not known failures and remain covered only by code review/pure tests or
CSS inspection:

- simultaneous same-gap races from two independently controlled voting clients,
  rejected action/seek/bonus-claim responses, autoplay refusal, deliberate slow
  buffering, and follower drift correction;
- a naturally grown ten-card/eleven-gap lane, equal-year inclusive tie reveal,
  zero-token voting, six-plus-token overflow, and game-over after ten cards;
- actual 200% browser zoom, touch/pen emulation, light mode,
  `prefers-contrast: more`, and `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`;
- stale-seat, manifest-error, and connection-loss injection;
- Leave room was visually present but not invoked because the controlled browser
  treats deletion of its persisted local seat as a destructive browser-data
  action.

One observable low-severity design deviation remains: the seven-player fallback
is ordered and usable, but its center draw block computes as `position: relative`
rather than the technical design's requested sticky center control. This does
not affect the supported two-through-six-player layout or game rules.
